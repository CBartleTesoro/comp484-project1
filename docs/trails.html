The Book:

The book is a small hand guide, with 4.25" x 6.87" dimentions, and is 159 pages long. The book contains:

  • Descriptions of 26 hikes, more tan 1000 kilometers of trails, among the 6000-meter peaks of the cordilleras of north-central Peru.
  • 16 pages of photographs.
  • 11 individual trail maps.
  • Foldout map of the entire region.
  • General information for foreign visitors on transportation, climate, mountain conditions, health, availability of supplies, the local people, etc.

Using this Guide

Most of the trail descriptions are divided into three parts:

  1. An Introduction — Information to help the hiker choose a trip:
    attractions, level of difficulty, trail conditions, etc.
  2. A Table of Distances and Altitutes — Distances between points along the trail are educated guesses; altitudes are estimated to the nearest 50m. Names of trails off the main route are indented and the distances and altitudes placed in parentheses
  3. A detailed route Description — How to follow the trail, places to camp, sources of water, etc. Sights are generally not described exept on side trips. Views are mentioned in a few cases where they serve as aides in following the route.

CAPITALS refer to places listed in the distance and altitude table above.

(xx,xx,xxxx) — km from previous point, total km, altitude, as listed in the table. (The first point along the trail has only one distance listed)

(xxxx) — Altitude in meters: altitudes given are those which are most generally accepted, ussually taken from Ingemmet maps. Altitudes ending in 00 or 50 are estimates (with the exeption of Nevado Pilanco, which apparently is exactly 5300m).

Italics indicate side trips off main route.

  • $ — US dollars; all prices are estimated in dollars(in which prices are more stable than in Peruvian soles).
    Note: Any $ estimate in the book is no longer accuarte, as it doesnt account for 46 years of inflation and economic changes.
  • km — kilometers (1km = 0.62 miles).
  • m — meters (1m = 3.28 feet.)
  • Route — a described course for hiking; may be a road, established trail, path, or completly trailless.
  • Main trail — principal route as described in this guide, altough other routes may be clear or more heavily traveled.
  • Alternate route — another route which leads to the same point.
  • Side trip — a route leading away from the main trail to a point of interest.
  • Cairn — trail marker, ussually a pile of rocks.
  • Switchback — zig-zag trail going up a steep slope.

The dificulty rating of the trails takes into account both the diffuclty of the terrain and the length of the hike. Altough my Standards may differ from yours, all the trails are judged comparatively one to another — if you walk one trail rated "moderate", you will know that another trail rated “moderate to easy” is somewhat easier. But no matter what your standard of dificulty is, you will find any trail rated “difficult” to be streneous. Be sure to you are adecuately prepared before making any of the following hikes: Pyramid of Alpamayo, Alto de Pucaraji, going over the Pasaje de Ulta, Laguna Legiacocha, and especially the circuit of the Cordillera Huayhuash.

Unhurried, leisruley walking covers about 3 km per hour. Steady, purposeful walking with occasional short rests is about 4 km per hour. It is diffucult to cover more than 5 km per hour even when walking as fast as possible and not sottping at all.
Burros ussually go a bit faster than 3km per hour, but unlike humans, do not slow noticeably when going up the steep parts.

Some rivers, lakes, passes and mountains have more than one name (or spelling of its name). The names used by local people will often be different from those in the book (some mountains have different names on every side). Probably half of the names on the Instituto Geografico Militar maps — names unheard of elsewhere — are different from those used here. I have used the names and spellings most commnly placed on other maps and used by people with extensive knowledge of the region.
